# Stern User's Guide

Stern keeps your notes, documents and passwords private. Everything is
locked with a password you choose and stays on your own computer. There
is no account, nothing is sent online, and nobody can read your
information without your password.

You use Stern like a chat: say what you want, and Stern asks a few short
questions. This guide walks through it from the first launch.

## 1. Create your password

The first time you open Stern, you create the one password that protects
everything you save.

![Create your password](User-Guide%20-%20Create%20Password.png)

1. Type a password in **Your password**. The list underneath ticks off as
   you meet each rule: at least 8 characters, a small letter, a capital
   letter, a number and a symbol.
2. Type the same password again in **Type it again**.
3. Tick the box to say you understand Stern is used at your own risk. You
   can tap **Read the disclaimer** first.
4. Tap **Continue**. Stern then reminds you to write your password down.
   Tap **I wrote it down** when you have.

> **Write your password down and keep it somewhere safe.** Nobody can
> get your information back if you forget it, not even the people who
> made Stern. That is what keeps it private.

## 2. Choose an AI model

Stern needs a small program, called an **AI model**, to understand what
you type. When you say "save a note", the AI model works out what you
mean and Stern does the rest. It runs on your computer, so nothing you
write leaves it.

The first time, Stern asks you to choose one:

![Choose an AI model](User-Guide%20-%20Choose%20AI%20Model.png)

There are two, and each one lists what you gain and what you give up:

| | Small (recommended) | Large |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Good | Replies quickly. Small download. Light on your computer's memory. | Understands more ways of saying things. Fewer misunderstandings. |
| To know | May misread longer or unusual wording. | Takes a few seconds to reply. Several times bigger to download. Uses more of your computer's memory. |
| Download | About 118 MB | About 483 MB |

**Not sure? Choose Small.** You can change it any time in Settings.

Tap **Download** next to the one you want. A bar shows how far along it
is. You only do this once, and it needs an internet connection. It is the
only time Stern uses the internet.

![A model downloading](User-Guide%20-%20Downloading.png)

When the download finishes, the chat opens.

## 3. Save a note

Notes are what Stern is mainly for: a shopping list, a will, a recipe,
anything you want kept private.

The chat starts with three buttons. Tap **Save a note**, or just type
`save a note`.

![The chat home screen](User-Guide%20-%20Chat%20Home.png)

Stern asks a few short questions, one at a time. First, a name:

![Stern asks what to call the note](User-Guide%20-%20Save%20A%20Note.png)

A box opens in the chat with a **Title** and a **Note** area. Write your
note there, then tap **Save note**. Tap **Cancel** to close the box
without saving.

To change a note later, type `edit my shopping list` (use your note's
name). The same box opens with the note in it. Chat starts and finds
notes; the box is where you write and change them.

Notes are not hidden. Passwords, card numbers and similar details are the
only things Stern masks.

![Finding a saved note](User-Guide%20-%20Find%20A%20Note.png)

## 4. Find something you saved

Type what you want to find, for example `find my shopping list`. Stern
looks it up and shows it.

![Finding a saved note](User-Guide%20-%20Find%20A%20Note.png)

If more than one thing matches, Stern lists them as buttons so you can
tap the one you mean.

## 5. Save a document or a password

- **A document** is longer text you paste in, like a letter or a
  contract. Tap **Save a document** (or type `save a document`) and Stern
  asks for a name, then for the text.
- **A password** is a username and password for a website. Tap **Save a
  password** (or type `save my GitHub password`). Stern asks for the
  website, the username, and then the password, which is private just
  like a note.

If you type `store` on its own, Stern asks what you would like to save:
a note, a document, a username and password, a credit card, or contact
info. You can tap a button or type its letter.

> **Documents are text only.** You can paste text, but you cannot attach
> a file yet. See [What Stern cannot do yet](#9-what-stern-cannot-do-yet).

## 6. Settings, light and dark, and Help

Along the top right are the lock switch, the question mark (Help) and
the gear (Settings).

### Light and dark

Open **Settings** and pick Light or Dark under **Look**. It works before
you sign in too, and Stern remembers your choice. Light is what you get
the first time.

![Stern in dark mode](User-Guide%20-%20Dark%20Theme.png)

### Settings

The gear opens Settings. **Look** is the light and dark choice. **AI
model** lets you download the other model, switch between them with
**Use this**, or free up space with **Remove**. Before you sign in, the
model list is shown but cannot be changed; unlock your vault to change it.
**Locking** has one choice, **Ask before locking**, which is on to begin
with. See [Lock Stern and open it again](#7-lock-stern-and-open-it-again).

![Settings](User-Guide%20-%20Settings.png)

### Help

The question mark opens Help, which answers common questions in a few
words. It is available at any time, including before you sign in.

![Help](User-Guide%20-%20Help.png)

## 7. Lock Stern and open it again

The switch at the top right shows **Unlocked** or **Locked**. When you
step away, turn the switch off. Stern asks **Lock Stern?**; tap **Lock**
to lock it, or **Cancel** to stay in. Stern then hides everything until
you enter your password again. It also locks when you close it.

If you would rather not be asked, open Settings and turn off **Ask before
locking**. The switch then locks Stern at once.

![Opening Stern with your password](User-Guide%20-%20Unlock.png)

Next time, type your password and tap **Open**. If you get it wrong five
times, Stern makes you wait five minutes before you can try again.

## 8. Keeping your information safe

- **Your password is the key.** Use one you do not use anywhere else, and
  write it down somewhere safe. There is no "forgot my password" button.
  If it is lost, the only way forward is to start over, which erases
  everything saved in Stern.
- **Keep your computer secure.** Stern locks your information, but it
  cannot protect a computer that someone else controls.
- **For anything you must never lose, keep a plain text file as well.**
  A text file ends in `.txt`, and every computer can open it with no
  special program, now or years from now. Nobody can shut it down or take
  it away. Keep it on your own computer, and keep a copy on a USB stick.
  Stern is a good place for things you want locked. A plain text file is
  best for things you must never lose.
- **Read the disclaimer.** It sets out what Stern does and does not
  promise. You can open it from the sign-in and setup screens, and from
  Help.

## 9. What Stern cannot do yet

Stern is still growing. Today it can **save** notes, documents and
passwords, and **find** them again. These are not available yet:

- Listing everything you have saved
- Changing or deleting something you saved
- Attaching a file (a document is pasted text)
- Choosing where to save an export, or importing one

If you ask for one of these, Stern may reply but will not do it. Planned
work is tracked on the project's
[issue list](https://github.com/christopheryang/stern/issues).

If Stern does not understand you, it suggests things to try, such as
`save a note`, `find my shopping list`, `save a document` and `help`.
Short, plain requests work best, and the **Small** AI model works well
with them.

If something does not work, see **Help** in the app, or the
[troubleshooting notes](../Runbook.md).
